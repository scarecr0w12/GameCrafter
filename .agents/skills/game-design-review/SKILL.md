# Game design review

Turn a creative brief into specific, testable design decisions while keeping the user in control of creative intent.

## Working agreement

- Follow the user’s current scope and existing authorization; use reversible defaults for routine choices.
- Read relevant project instructions, decisions, and current artifacts before changing their meaning.
- Separate user-confirmed requirements, proposed defaults, implementation, and verification evidence.
- Use actual installed tools and documented interfaces; record missing capabilities explicitly.
- Keep durable Project/task/board records in the platform service through supported contracts.
- Read [scenario workflows](references/workflows.md) when preparing examples, handoffs, or verification.

## Inputs to establish

- Player audience, genre, intended emotions, and core actions.
- Current brief or prototype, supported platforms, session length, and constraints.
- Known feedback with participant context; distinguish observation from interpretation.

## Procedure

### 1. Read the design contract

- Extract the promised experience and explicit non-goals.
- Separate user-confirmed choices from assumptions and proposed defaults.

### 2. Map the playable loop

- Describe action, decision, feedback, consequence, and the next decision.
- Include failure, recovery, and the reason to repeat without relying on cosmetic rewards.

### 3. Inspect meaningful choices

- Compare available actions by cost, risk, information, and payoff.
- Find dominant options, false choices, and missing feedback using concrete examples.

### 4. Review progression and economy

- Trace resource sources, sinks, unlock requirements, and reset rules.
- Use small worked examples to inspect pacing; label hypothetical numbers as proposed tuning.

### 5. Check teachability

- Follow a first-time player through the first action and first failure.
- Identify what the game demonstrates, what it explains, and what it expects players to discover.

### 6. Check the experience under constraints

- Inspect input, camera, readability, interruption, and session length.
- Route accessibility barriers to game-accessibility with affected scenarios.

### 7. Choose a prototype question

- Select the uncertainty most likely to change the design.
- Choose paper, greybox, or playable code according to the question, and define success observations.

### 8. Plan an observation session

- Give participants a task without coaching the intended solution.
- Record behavior and confusion separately from their opinions; report participant limitations.

### 9. Prioritize revisions

- Rank findings by player impact and confidence, then estimate change size.
- Tie each proposed change to one observed problem or clearly named hypothesis.

### 10. Deliver a reviewable decision

- Provide keep/change/remove recommendations and the next smallest test.
- Do not declare a design fun or balanced from a document review alone.

## Deliverables

- Experience contract and loop description.
- Prioritized findings with examples, confidence, and rationale.
- Prototype hypothesis, observation plan, and proposed revision.

## Completion review

- Every criticism references the promised experience or a stated constraint.
- Tuning recommendations show at least one worked player scenario.
- A prototype plan names an uncertainty and observable result.
- Playtest claims identify participant context and actual observation artifacts.

## Gotchas

- A fashionable genre convention may undermine the requested experience.
- High engagement numbers can hide frustration or compulsory repetition.
- Agent playthroughs do not substitute for human preference evidence.
