# Game design review: scenario workflows

These are original worked recommendations. Fixtures and identifiers are hypothetical; substitute actual project contracts and supported tools.
Last researched: 2026-10-01.

## Worked request

**Request:** A crafting loop feels repetitive.

**Initial investigation:** Review the brief and sample session; compare recipe decisions with collection costs.

**Proposed action:** Propose one prototype where materials force a tradeoff, then observe whether players change plans.

**Expected handoff:** A loop map, three prioritized findings, and a prototype test; enjoyment remains a hypothesis.

## Detailed scenarios

### 1. Test the action loop

- Use a hypothetical five-minute scavenging session: collect scrap, choose an upgrade, enter a riskier room, then return to safety.

- Map every choice to information available before commitment; mark outcomes that become legible only after failure.

- Compare a cautious route with a fast route using the same starting equipment and resource values.

- If both routes yield identical decisions, propose a change to risk, information, or reward rather than adding another currency.

- Record where a player waits, repeats without deciding, or misses feedback; capture timestamps and input context.

- Deliver a loop diagram annotated with observed behavior and a separate column of designer interpretations.

### 2. Stress a proposed economy

- Create a hypothetical ledger: earn 8 scrap per run, repair costs 3, upgrade costs 20, and a failed run loses carried scrap.

- Work through three successful runs, two failures followed by recovery, and an expert player who avoids repairs.

- Look for a state where the player cannot afford the action needed to earn more; identify any recovery source.

- Check whether one purchase dominates because it increases income and survivability with no competing cost.

- Compare a changed repair price or alternate source in the same scenarios; label values as illustrative tuning.

- Keep source/sink tables and changed decisions, not a claim that the economy is balanced without playtests.

### 3. Observe onboarding without coaching

- Give a new participant the objective Find shelter before night, with the planned opening UI and no walkthrough.

- Record their first attempted action, mistaken control assumptions, and whether they notice the shelter cue.

- Let one nonfatal mistake occur before intervening; record the intervention because it changes subsequent evidence.

- Ask the participant what they expected after the action, then distinguish their explanation from recorded behavior.

- Propose a demonstrative cue or earlier safe practice opportunity when information is missing; preserve deliberate discovery.

- Save task wording, participant context, timestamps, and the exact version so later tests can use comparable conditions.

### 4. Choose the next discriminating prototype

- If uncertainty concerns whether limited ammunition creates choices, prototype the encounter before polishing weapon models.

- Prepare two versions differing only in ammunition availability and retain the same enemy placement and damage.

- Observe resource use, route changes, and retreat decisions; do not define success only as time played.

- If participants exploit an unintended mechanic, classify whether it is useful emergent play or a broken contract.

- Compare design benefit with content and usability costs; a more difficult variant is not automatically better.

- Recommend keep, revise, or discard with confidence and the observations that would change that recommendation.

## Reviewable handoff

Include the requested result and the evidence specific to the scenarios above:

- Experience contract and loop description.
- Prioritized findings with examples, confidence, and rationale.
- Prototype hypothesis, observation plan, and proposed revision.

Keep proposed numbers, hypothetical fixtures, and unexecuted checks visible in the handoff. An authored plan or documented mechanism does not imply a successful runtime result.

## Acceptance assertions

- Every criticism references the promised experience or a stated constraint.
- Tuning recommendations show at least one worked player scenario.
- A prototype plan names an uncertainty and observable result.
- Playtest claims identify participant context and actual observation artifacts.
